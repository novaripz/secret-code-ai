Subject: Minecraft setup - 6 steps, works on your existing world


Here's the whole thing. Six steps, about 20 minutes, most of it just waiting on
downloads. It works on the world you already play - you don't have to start over.
You quit the game once, at step 2.

You need Minecraft for Windows (Bedrock Edition) - the one you already play with
your friends.


THE STEPS
---------

1. Download the pack and unzip it:
   https://github.com/novaripz/secret-code-ai/archive/refs/heads/claude/bedrock-streamer-pack-bs2c1j.zip

   Right-click the ZIP > Extract All. Open the "streamer-minecraft" folder inside.
   Keep it on your Desktop.

2. Open Minecraft, load the world you want to use, and check two settings.
   In world settings turn on "Multiplayer Game" and "Require players to accept
   resource packs". This can be your existing survival world - nothing gets wiped
   and a full backup is taken automatically before anything is touched.
   Then quit Minecraft completely.

3. Double-click START-HERE.cmd
   It opens all the download pages in your browser.

4. Click the green Download button on every page.
   Leave the files in your Downloads folder. Don't rename them, don't unzip them,
   don't move them.

5. Go back to the setup window and press Enter.
   It shows you a list of your worlds - pick the one from step 2. It installs
   everything, switches it on in that world, and opens Minecraft for you.

6. In Minecraft: Settings > Video > Graphics Mode > Vibrant Visuals.
   Open your world. Play.

Your friends join from Play > Friends while your world is open and tap accept on
the download prompt. They install nothing.


ONE THING ABOUT USING AN OLD WORLD
----------------------------------

The new biomes, caves and structures only appear in land you haven't explored yet.
Your base and everything around it stays exactly as it is - walk a few thousand
blocks in any direction and the new stuff starts showing up. Everything else (the
visuals, the animals, the cats, the ore boost, the extra dimension) works
everywhere immediately, old chunks included.


THE TWO HORROR ADDONS - KEEP THEM OR BIN THEM
---------------------------------------------

Two of the downloads are horror. They're installed but they're entirely your call:

  VERITY - an entity you can actually talk to. Put it on the ground and type in
  chat, or keep it in your inventory and say "Hey Verity". It answers.

  NULL - you don't summon it. It turns up in your world on its own after a while,
  and messes with things: sounds, signs, the time of day, animals going missing.

To keep both: do nothing, they're already on.

  9a-REMOVE-NULL-ONLY.cmd     removes Null, keeps Verity
  9b-REMOVE-VERITY-ONLY.cmd   removes Verity, keeps Null
  9-HORROR-OFF.cmd            removes both
  10-HORROR-ON.cmd            puts them back

Two honest notes on them:

* Achievements are off in this world because of Verity - it needs Minecraft's
  "Beta APIs" setting, and no world can have that and achievements at the same
  time. Removing Verity later does NOT bring them back; once a world has had that
  setting on, it's flagged forever. So if achievements matter to you, run
  9b-REMOVE-VERITY-ONLY.cmd before you play, or use a different world.

* Null's last update was built for an older version of Minecraft, so it might
  simply not do anything on the current one. Everything else in the pack is
  current. If nothing has happened after a long session, that's why - remove it
  with 9a and you've lost nothing.

THE BUTTONS YOU'LL ACTUALLY USE
-------------------------------

  3-BEAUTIFUL-MODE.cmd    full atmosphere
  4-PERFORMANCE-MODE.cmd  same addons, better FPS
  11-OPTIMIZE-STREAM.cmd  tunes the game for a steady 60 while live
  5-BACKUP-WORLD.cmd      saves a copy of your world right now
  6-RESTORE-BACKUP.cmd    puts an old copy back if anything ever goes wrong

Close Minecraft before running any of them, open it again after. Each one takes a
backup first, so nothing is permanent.

IF SOMETHING LOOKS OFF
----------------------
Double-click 1-CHECK-SETUP.cmd - it tells you what's installed and what's on.
It's almost always one of these three:

  Minecraft was open while you ran a script  ->  close it and run the script again
  You picked the wrong world from the list   ->  run it again, pick the right one
  No aurora in the sky                       ->  Settings > Video > Graphics Mode
